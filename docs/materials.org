#+subtitle: whelk documentation
#+options: toc:nil num:nil

Frosted glass, and the half of it the compositor has to do.

* Who blurs what

A Wayland surface cannot blur what is behind it.  The layer-shell protocol has
no backdrop filter, and a client that tried to fake one would have to read the
screen back, which no compositor allows.  Every blurred bar, launcher or
notification panel you have seen on Wayland is blurred by its *compositor*, told
which layer surfaces to touch by their namespace.

What whelk can paint is the glass over that blur: a warm translucent tint, two
soft gradients that catch the light like a pane, a grain that keeps a blurred
backdrop from banding, a hairline along the top edge, and a shadow under panes
that float.  That is the =frosted-glass= finish.  Because every surface's skin
is generated in one place, one setting reaches the bar, the launcher, the
desktop widget cards, the notification centre and its toasts, the network panel
and the on-screen display together.

The bar's =#:background= alpha is a bar setting, not a panel one.  A menu, a
launcher or the network panel is a sheet of content read over whatever is
behind it, and layer-shell has no backdrop filter for those surfaces; at the
bar's 0.68 the calendar showed through the Window menu and the network list wore
the wallpaper's stripes as row separators.  Whelk therefore raises a floating
surface's background to at least 0.94 alpha while leaving the bar, the widget
cards and a deliberately transparent colour alone.  The bar keeps its glass;
the things that have to be *read* stay legible over any wallpaper.

| Part of the look                     | Painted by   | Set by                                |
|--------------------------------------+--------------+---------------------------------------|
| the blur of the wallpaper behind it   | the compositor | the layer rule in this document     |
| tint and opacity                      | whelk        | =#:frost= / the bar's =#:opacity=     |
| the two warm gradients                | whelk        | =#:warmth=                            |
| the grain                             | whelk        | =#:grain=                             |
| the hairline along the top edge       | whelk        | =#:highlight=                         |
| the shadow under floating panes       | whelk        | =#:shadow=                            |
| corner radius                         | whelk        | the bar's =#:radius=, a theme's radius |

The two halves have to agree on the corner radius: whelk rounds its own surface,
and the compositor rounds the *blur* to the =geometry-corner-radius= you give
its layer rule.  A rule that says 0 leaves a square blur behind a rounded bar,
which reads as a halo.

* The finish whelk paints

The finish is a shell setting, next to the wallpaper policy:

#+begin_src scheme
  (configure-shell!
   #:bars (list (bar #:name 'main #:height 32 #:opacity 0.85
                     #:left (list (menu-module))
                     #:right (list (clock-module))))
   #:frost (frosted-glass #:tint "#17110d"
                          #:opacity 0.62
                          #:warmth 0.35))
#+end_src

| Key          | Default | What it does                                                       |
|--------------+---------+--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =#:enabled?= | =#t=    | whether the finish is painted at all                               |
| =#:tint=     | =""=    | a CSS colour laid over the surface's own background                |
| =#:opacity=  | =0.62=  | the tint's alpha                                                    |
| =#:warmth=   | =0.35=  | how strongly the two warm gradients show                            |
| =#:grain=    | =0.06=  | the frosted grain; it also hides banding in a blurred backdrop      |
| =#:highlight= | =0.30=  | the hairline along the top edge, and a faint border                 |
| =#:shadow=   | =0.35=  | the soft shadow under floating panes                                |

An empty =#:tint= keeps every surface's configured colour -- the bar's
=#:background=, the theme's background, the panel's own -- and adds only the
gradients, the grain and the edges.  That is the way to warm up an existing
palette without repainting it, and it is what a settings file that says
=(frosted-glass)= gets.

All the numbers are clamped to =0.0 .. 1.0=.  A settings file that never mentions
=#:frost= has the finish off, so nothing changes for a machine that upgrades
into it.  A finish that cannot be read (a typo in the block) logs one warning
and leaves the shell flat rather than refusing to start.

** Turning the finish off

#+begin_src scheme
  #:frost (frosted-glass #:enabled? #f)
#+end_src

Leaving =#:frost= out entirely does the same for a settings file that never had
it.  Both keep the compositor rule harmless: a fully opaque surface simply has
nothing to show through.

** The finish and the bar's own material

The bar's =#:background=, =#:opacity=, =#:shine=, =#:blur=, =#:reflection= and
=#:flare= still apply.  The frost gradients are painted *under* the bar's own
effects, so an explicitly configured shine keeps its place on top, and a
configured =#:shine= replaces the frost's top hairline instead of stacking two
highlights on the same edge.  A configured =#:border-color= wins over the
frost's faint border.

Opacity is what lets a blur show at all.  A bar at =#:opacity 1.0= is opaque no
matter what the compositor blurs behind it; =0.75= to =0.9= shows the wallpaper
through a dark tint, and below roughly =0.5= text starts to fight the desktop
behind it.

A floating layer surface is exactly its card.  The compositor blurs the whole
surface geometry, so transparent space between a surface's edge and the card
inside it is blurred desktop showing as a band around the card, and a
compositor-drawn shadow follows that wider rectangle.  The launcher, the network
panel, the notification centre and the toast stack therefore size the surface
to the card itself.  The gap you see around a panel is a layer-shell placement
margin, which sits outside the surface; it is not padding inside the geometry.

* Layer namespaces

The compositor matches layer surfaces by namespace.  whelk sets one per
surface, so a rule can blur the chrome and leave the wallpaper alone:

| Namespace                    | Surface                                        |
|------------------------------+------------------------------------------------|
| =whelk-rust=                 | the bar                                        |
| =whelk-rust-launcher=        | the launcher                                   |
| =whelk-rust-notifications=   | the notification centre                        |
| =whelk-rust-toasts=          | the notification toast stack                   |
| =whelk-rust-network=         | the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth panel                  |
| =whelk-rust-osd=             | the volume and brightness display              |
| =whelk-widget=               | each desktop widget card                       |
| =whelk-rust-wallpaper=       | the wallpaper surface (opaque, never blurred)  |
| =whelk-wallpaper-picker=     | the wallpaper picker                           |
| =hasp-lock=                  | the lock screen (hasp, a separate project)     |

=whelk-rust= (the bar) and =whelk-widget= are the two a plain prefix rule
misses; the wallpaper surface is the one worth excluding.

* niri

Layer rules arrived in niri 25.01 and the =background-effect= block in 26.04, so
blur behind a layer surface needs a niri that new.  Put this in =config.kdl=:

#+begin_src kdl
  layer-rule {
      match namespace="^(whelk-rust|whelk-widget)"
      exclude namespace="^whelk-rust-wallpaper$"

      background-effect {
          blur true
          noise 0.04
          saturation 1.1
      }

      // Match the bar's radius, or the blur corners will not.
      geometry-corner-radius 12
  }
#+end_src

- =noise= adds a little dither to the blur.  whelk already paints its own grain;
  keep this small (=0.02= to =0.05=) or drop it, and let =#:grain= do the work.
- =saturation= above 1 makes the wallpaper's colours read through the tint.
- The rule applies to every whelk layer surface the regexes match, so the
  launcher, both panels, the toasts and the OSD are covered by the same block.
- niri's =shadow { on }= in the same rule draws a shadow around the whole
  surface.  whelk keeps that surface exactly the card and places it with
  layer-shell margins, so the shadow hugs the card; the bar's shadow is already
  painted by whelk.

List what niri sees with =niri msg layers=, which prints each surface's
namespace and is the fastest way to check a regex.

* Hyprland

Hyprland has enabled blur for layer surfaces for a long time.  The current
configuration form is =hl.layer_rule=:

#+begin_src lua
  hl.layer_rule({
      match = { namespace = "^(whelk-rust|whelk-widget)" },
      blur = true,
      ignore_alpha = 0.5,
  })
#+end_src

Older =.conf= configurations spell the same thing as one line per effect:

#+begin_src ini
  layerrule = blur, whelk-rust
  layerrule = blur, whelk-rust-launcher
  layerrule = blur, whelk-rust-notifications
  layerrule = blur, whelk-rust-toasts
  layerrule = blur, whelk-rust-network
  layerrule = blur, whelk-rust-osd
  layerrule = blur, whelk-widget
  layerrule = ignorealpha 0.5, whelk-rust
#+end_src

- =ignore_alpha= keeps the blur from painting over the fully transparent pixels
  outside the rounded corners; without it a rounded pane gets square blur
  corners.
- Blur strength and passes are global in Hyprland (=blur:size=, =blur:passes=,
  =blur:new_optimizations=); the layer rule only says *which* layers blur.
- =hyprctl layers= lists namespaces and their rectangles.

* SwayFX

SwayFX matches layer namespaces exactly, one =layer_effects= block per
namespace:

#+begin_src ini
  blur enable
  blur_radius 6
  blur_passes 2

  layer_effects "whelk-rust" {
      blur enable;
      blur_ignore_transparent enable;
      corner_radius 12;
  }

  layer_effects "whelk-rust-launcher" {
      blur enable;
      blur_ignore_transparent enable;
      corner_radius 12;
  }
#+end_src

Repeat the block for =whelk-rust-notifications=, =whelk-rust-toasts=,
=whelk-rust-network=, =whelk-rust-osd= and =whelk-widget=.  A =layer_effects=
block accepts only =blur=, =blur_xray=, =blur_ignore_transparent=, =shadows=,
=corner_radius= and =reset=; anything else makes sway log =Invalid layer_effects
effect= and drop the whole line, so set =blur_radius=, =blur_passes= and
=blur_noise= globally instead.  =blur_ignore_transparent enable= is what keeps
the blur inside the rounded corners.

=swaymsg -r -t get_outputs | jq '.[0].layer_shell_surfaces | .[] | .namespace'=
prints the namespaces in use.

* Compositors without a blur

sway (without FX), river, labwc, Weston and most wlroots compositors have no
backdrop blur, and nothing whelk does can add it.  Two options:

1. Keep the finish and raise the tint's opacity so the panels read as warm
   glass over the wallpaper rather than as a hole in it:
   =(frosted-glass #:tint "#17110d" #:opacity 0.9 #:warmth 0.5)=.
2. Turn the finish off and use an opaque material, which is what those
   compositors' own panels do.

The gradients and the grain cost nothing either way -- they are part of the
surface's CSS, not a filter -- so a machine without compositor blur still gets
the warm pane, just without the wallpaper's colours moving underneath it.

* Three recipes

** Warm dark glass, the house look

#+begin_src scheme
  (bar #:name 'main #:theme 'ember #:opacity 0.86 #:radius 12
       #:left (list (menu-module))
       #:right (list (clock-module)))

  #:frost (frosted-glass #:tint "#17110d" #:opacity 0.62 #:warmth 0.40
                         #:grain 0.06 #:highlight 0.30 #:shadow 0.35)
#+end_src

Amber-ish gradients over a near-black tint: the desktop shows through, the
gradients read as light falling on the pane.

** Pale glass

#+begin_src scheme
  #:frost (frosted-glass #:tint "#f4ece1" #:opacity 0.55 #:warmth 0.20
                         #:grain 0.04 #:highlight 0.45 #:shadow 0.20)
#+end_src

For a light palette or a bright wallpaper; a stronger hairline keeps the edges
from dissolving.

** No tint at all

#+begin_src scheme
  #:frost (frosted-glass #:warmth 0.25 #:grain 0.05)
#+end_src

Keeps every surface's colour and adds only the warm wash, the grain and the
edges.  This is the setting to try first on an existing bar: it cannot change
what the palette already decided.

* When the glass does not show

| What you see                                  | What to check                                                      |
|-----------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| no blur at all                                 | the compositor rule exists; =niri msg layers= / =hyprctl layers= show the namespace |
| a square blur behind a rounded bar             | =geometry-corner-radius= / =corner_radius= is 0 or too small       |
| the bar looks flat, no tint                    | =#:frost= missing, =#:enabled? #f=, or =#:tint= empty by intent      |
| the bar is opaque                              | the bar or theme =#:opacity= is 1.0                                 |
| blur flickers or the panel is grey             | the compositor fell back to software rendering                      |
| =Invalid layer_effects effect= in sway's log   | a global-only option was put inside =layer_effects=                 |

A blur pass costs the compositor a downscale and a few shader passes per frame
in the area it covers.  A bar and an occasional panel are cheap; a rule that
matches the full-screen wallpaper surface is not, which is why the recipes
exclude it.
