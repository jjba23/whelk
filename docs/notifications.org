#+subtitle: whelk documentation

whelk owns =org.freedesktop.Notifications= when nothing else does: toasts, a centre grouped by application, actions and do-not-disturb.

whelk owns =org.freedesktop.Notifications= when no other daemon has it (one
that already owns the name is left alone, with one line in the log saying so).
It advertises
=body-markup=, =actions=, =icon-static= and =persistence=, keeps the history, and
shows a notification twice:

- as a *toast*, top right, five at most (the reference bar's visible limit),
  leaving on the application's timeout -- ten seconds when it
  does not say, and not at all for a critical one (a transient notification
  also leaves the store then);
- in the *centre*, a panel on the right edge, grouped by the application that
  sent it -- its icon and its count over each group -- newest first, with the
  application's actions as buttons, a dismiss per notification, =Clear all= and
  do-not-disturb; an empty centre says so instead of showing an empty panel.

A toast carries up to two of the notification's action buttons as well: a
=Reply= that only appears after opening the centre is a button that arrives too
late.  The centre's header counts what is stored: "3 notifications" over the
groups, not hidden in a tooltip.

The bell (=notifications-module=) marks what is stored; the count is in its
hover text and in the centre, not printed on the bar.  A left click opens the
centre, a right click toggles do-not-disturb.  With DND on, notifications are
still stored and counted; they simply do not raise a toast.

Three rules the diagram is really saying:

- The store is the truth.  A toast is a view of it, so closing a toast loses
  nothing, and opening the centre later shows what arrived while it was closed.
- The timeout belongs to the sender.  With no =expire_timeout= the toast takes
  ten seconds; a critical notification has no timeout; a transient one leaves
  the store when its toast leaves.
- Do-not-disturb is a flag on the [[file:idle.org][idle manager]], not a filter in the
  store, so turning it off shows everything that arrived while it was on.  It
  deliberately does not pause the lock timer (see [[file:idle.org][Idle]]).
